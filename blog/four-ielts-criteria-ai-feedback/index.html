Decoding the Four Official IELTS Assessment Criteria Through AI Feedback

Stuck at Band 6.5? The missing piece isn't more practice — it's understanding how the scoring matrix dissects your output.

Moving beyond general impressions

When human or automated systems grade an English proficiency assessment, they do not look at an essay or speech sample as a single, indivisible block. Instead, they apply a multi-dimensional rubric that isolates specific linguistic competencies. Understanding these pillars allows you to target your study time effectively.

The four pillars in detail

  • Task Achievement / Task Response: This criterion measures how directly and completely you answer the prompt. In writing or speaking, missing a sub-question or failing to support a main position caps your score regardless of how good your grammar is.
  • Coherence & Cohesion: This assesses the architecture of your response. AI scans for logical paragraph breaks, smooth transitions, and the precise use of cohesive devices to guide the reader or listener seamlessly through your argument.
  • Lexical Resource: Vocabulary scoring goes far beyond using "big words." Algorithms evaluate lexical breadth, collocation naturalness, and precision — rewarding precise word choices over forced, unnatural synonyms.
  • Grammatical Range & Accuracy: This looks at your structural versatility (mixing simple, compound, and complex sentences) weighed against the density and severity of syntactic errors.

How AI feedback accelerates improvement

Traditional grading often leaves students with a vague score and little direction on why they lost points. Diagnostic AI breaks down feedback per criterion, highlighting precisely where cohesion faltered or where lexical range was restricted, enabling targeted revisions.

Frequently asked questions

Which of the four criteria is weighted most heavily?

All four criteria are weighted equally in your final band score for IELTS Writing. Your overall score is the average of the four, rounded to the nearest 0.5 or whole band.

Can I score Band 8 on Lexical Resource with weak grammar?

No — your overall band is the average. A high Lexical Resource score will be dragged down by weak Grammar. The highest band 9 scorers are strong across all four pillars.

Does the Speaking test use the same four criteria?

Speaking uses four related criteria: Fluency & Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range & Accuracy, and Pronunciation (replacing Task Response).
