Computer-Delivered vs Paper-Based Tests: Interface Tips and Time Management

IELTS Maestro Team·Oct 23, 2026·5 min read

Shifting to a digital screen changes everything. Are you ready for computer-delivered exams?

In this post

  1. Adapting to the digital interface
  2. Time management behind the screen
  3. FAQ

Adapting to the digital interface

Transitioning from pen and paper to a screen-based assessment introduces entirely new mechanical and cognitive challenges.

  • Building muscle memory: Familiarize yourself with digital highlighting, note-taking tools, and word-count trackers so you do not waste precious seconds figuring out the software on test day.
  • Combating screen fatigue: Reading dense academic passages on a monitor can cause eye strain and cognitive drain. Practice pacing your breaks and maintaining focus during long reading modules.

Time management behind the screen

Digital interfaces offer distinct advantages — such as running countdown timers and automatic word counts — but they also demand strict pacing discipline.

  • Typing speed vs handwriting: If your typing speed is slow, your writing tasks will suffer. Practice touch-typing timed essays to ensure you can easily hit word counts with time to spare for editing.
  • Navigation discipline: Avoid clicking back and forth aimlessly between sections. Use the review screen to flag uncertain answers and return to them systematically.

Frequently asked questions

Is the computer-delivered IELTS easier or harder than paper?

The questions are identical. Many candidates find the computer version easier because of the running timer, auto-word-count, and clearer navigation — but only if their typing speed is above 30 words per minute.

What's a safe typing speed for the Writing section?

Aim for at least 30–35 words per minute. Below 25 wpm, you'll struggle to hit the 250-word Task 2 minimum within 40 minutes while leaving time to edit.

Do I get the same preparation time on computer-delivered?

Yes — all timings, preview windows, and section lengths are the same. The only mechanical difference is how you input your answers.
